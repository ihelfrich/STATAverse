How these data are generated

Independent standard-normal draws z, u and ε generate x = ρz + √(1 − ρ²)u, then y = 2 + βx + γz + σε. The known direct effect is β in this constructed system. Both x and z have unit population variance.

The adjusted model has the correct specification here by construction. Adding a variable to a real regression does not establish that it is an appropriate control or remove all confounding.

What “holding z fixed” does to the data

Fit x on z and save its residuals. Do the same for y. Regressing those residuals on each other recovers the coefficient of x from the full regression. All three steps use the same observations and include an intercept when removing z.

The slope identity does not transfer the residual regression’s default standard error. The uncertainty shown here comes from the full model, with n − 3 residual degrees of freedom and the selected covariance estimator.

Where the unadjusted slope comes from

In this sample, the unadjusted slope equals the adjusted x coefficient plus the fitted z coefficient multiplied by the slope from regressing z on x. This is an exact least-squares decomposition, including sampling variation.

How much does one observation matter?

Leverage measures an unusual combination of x and z. A residual measures distance from the fitted surface. An observation can have one without the other; Cook’s distance combines both.
