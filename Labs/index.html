Analysis labs

Each lab takes one part of empirical work and lets you change the data underneath it. Four are experiments on small simulated datasets. The fifth is a whole project, carried out in a Stata session that runs in this page. Every lab runs in your browser, uploads nothing, and exports the data and the Stata commands it used, so the work can be repeated in Stata.

  1. 01

    Open experiment

    Regression and adjustment

    Set the direct effects of x and z, their correlation and the noise, and draw a sample. The lab fits the regression with and without z, shows the same observations in the raw scatterplot and in the partial-regression plot, splits the gap between the two coefficients into its omitted-variable parts, and lets you take one observation out to see how far the estimates depend on it.

    You work with
    A simulated sample. You choose the parameters, the sample size and the seed.
    Time
    About 20 minutes.
    Before you start
    The regression lesson in Practice, if regression output is new to you.
    In Stata
    regress, avplot, predict for leverage and Cook's distance.

  2. 02

    Open experiment

    Uncertainty and repeated samples

    Fit one regression with conventional, heteroskedasticity-robust and cluster-robust standard errors. Then draw up to 2,000 new samples from the same population and count how often each interval contains the coefficient you set, with the Monte Carlo error of that count.

    You work with
    Simulated grouped data. You choose the number of groups, the rows in each and how the errors behave.
    Time
    About 25 minutes.
    Before you start
    The inference lesson in Practice. Lab 01 helps but is not required.
    In Stata
    regress with vce(robust) and vce(cluster), simulate.

  3. 03

    Three guided investigations, or open exploration

    Merge and reshape

    Follow single records from their source tables to the result. Change the keys, the relationship you declare or the layout, and see which rows and cells Stata would keep, which keys are duplicated or unmatched, and where two sources disagree about the same value.

    You work with
    Small built-in tables, such as orders and customers, or scores and attendance by term.
    Time
    About 15 minutes for each investigation; 17 checked tasks in all.
    Before you start
    The lesson on keys and duplicate records in Practice.
    In Stata
    merge 1:1, m:1 and 1:m, reshape long and wide, isid.

  4. 04

    Three guided investigations, or open exploration

    Instrumental variables

    Follow an instrument from the first stage and the reduced form to the IV ratio. Weaken the instrument, or let it affect the outcome directly, and compare what two-stage least squares and the Anderson–Rubin confidence set report with the coefficient you set.

    You work with
    A simulated sample. You choose the strength of the instrument, any exclusion failure, the sample size and the seed.
    Time
    About 20 minutes for each investigation; 16 checked tasks in all.
    Before you start
    The omitted-variable argument in lab 01.
    In Stata
    ivregress 2sls, with the first stage and reduced form as regress.

  5. 05

    Guided project in twelve checked steps

    A firm-year panel, from merged file to results table

    Take a merged firm-year file to a table of five models: lags under xtset, a sample defined once, winsorizing by hand, a clustered regression, firm fixed effects and a Poisson model that keeps the zeros. Each step gives you Stata lines to complete, runs them in a session that carries over from step to step, and checks the result in the data.

    You work with
    A firm-year panel simulated from a fixed seed, or your own .dta or .csv file with the same columns. Your file is read in this browser and is not uploaded.
    Time
    Two to three hours. Progress on the simulated panel is kept in this browser.
    Before you start
    The lesson on reproducible do-files in Practice. Labs 01 to 03 cover the ideas it uses.
    In Stata
    xtset, egen, _pctile, regress with vce(cluster), areg, poisson, estimates table, logit, margins.

Choosing where to start

The numbers suggest an order; they do not impose one. Labs 01 and 02 belong together: the first is about what a coefficient measures, the second about how far to trust it. Lab 03 is about preparing data and can be done at any point. Lab 04 assumes the omitted-variable argument of lab 01. Lab 05 draws on all of them and is the only lab in which you write the Stata yourself.

Checked tasks in labs 03 and 04 are stored in this browser, and Saved work can back them up or restore them.
