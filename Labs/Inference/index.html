Reproducible analysis

The corresponding Stata commands

Export the experiment and extract it into a project folder. The sample do-file imports the exact displayed observations. The native simulation generates new draws from the same population design; Stata's random-number stream differs from the browser's.

What the experiment assumes

All groups are independent and equal in size. The predictor and the errors have independent sources, so E(error | x) = 0. Every fit includes an intercept and one predictor; no weights, missing observations or omitted confounders enter this experiment.

Population generator

For group g and row i, x = √ρₓ ag + √(1 − ρₓ) vgi. The error is σs(x)[√ρₑ bg + √(1 − ρₑ) εgi], where s(x) = exp(hx/2 − h²/4). All a, b, v and ε draws are independent standard normals. The normalization keeps the population mean error variance at σ² as h changes. Conditional on x, the within-group error correlation is ρₑ; with varying s(x), unconditional correlation need not equal that value.

Changing a parameter retains the underlying random draws at a fixed seed and sample number. Repeated-sample runs start at sample 1. The browser seed does not reproduce Stata's random-number stream; export the observations for an exact comparison.

Covariance formulas and reference distributions

Conventional inference uses SSE/(N − 2) and assumes independent errors with constant conditional variance. HC1 sums individual squared score contributions and multiplies by N/(N − 2). CR1 sums scores within each group before squaring, then multiplies by [G/(G − 1)]·[(N − 1)/(N − 2)]. Both sandwich formulas use the same OLS residuals.

Conventional and HC1 intervals use t critical values with N − 2 degrees of freedom. CR1 uses G − 1. These match the corresponding unweighted Stata regress options. Normal, independent, constant-variance errors give conventional t inference an exact conditional justification. HC1 and CR1 rely on approximations; the degrees-of-freedom correction does not make them exact.

What changing the covariance cannot do

Robust or clustered inference does not remove omitted-variable bias, establish a causal design, or decide which grouping is appropriate for an empirical study. This generator supplies the grouping and exogeneity. With few independent groups, uneven information or other departures, the usual cluster correction can perform poorly. A clustered standard error can also be smaller than an unclustered one in a particular sample.
