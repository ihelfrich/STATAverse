## Welcome to the Reading Library

This collection is the working reference library for applied econometrics and Stata. If you are new to quantitative methods, start with the intuition-first chapters. If you are writing for publication, use the diagnostics and design sections to sharpen your methods.

Each recommendation has been selected for clarity, practical relevance, and alignment with modern research standards. The library is organized by topic, with clear guidance on how texts complement one another and when to use each resource.

---

## Essential Textbook Collection

### Foundations of Econometrics

**Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach** (7th ed., 2020)
*Jeffrey M. Wooldridge*

The gold standard for undergraduate and masters-level econometrics. Wooldridge's intuitive explanations and emphasis on real-world applications make complex concepts accessible without sacrificing rigor. Exceptional for building foundational understanding of OLS, inference, specification, and basic panel methods.

**A Guide to Econometrics** (6th ed., 2008)
*Peter Kennedy*

The indispensable companion to any technical econometrics course. Kennedy translates mathematical formalism into plain language, providing the intuition behind estimators that textbooks often obscure. Outstanding for understanding "why" before diving into "how."

### Advanced Econometric Theory

**Econometric Analysis** (8th ed., 2018)
*William H. Greene*

The encyclopedic reference for graduate econometrics. Comprehensive coverage from finite-sample theory to asymptotic methods, cross-sectional to time-series, frequentist to Bayesian approaches. Essential for doctoral students and researchers requiring rigorous theoretical foundations.

**Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data** (2nd ed., 2010)
*Jeffrey M. Wooldridge*

The definitive graduate-level treatment of microeconometric methods. Rigorous yet accessible coverage of linear models, endogeneity, panel data, binary choice, censored data, and count models. The go-to reference for applied microeconomists.

### Stata Implementation

**Microeconometrics Using Stata** (2nd ed., Vols. I & II, 2022)
*A. Colin Cameron and Pravin K. Trivedi*

The most comprehensive guide to implementing modern microeconometric methods in Stata. Volume I covers foundations, linear models, simulation, and basic nonlinear models. Volume II addresses endogeneity, treatment effects, count data, and more advanced topics. Indispensable for translating theory into practice.

**An Introduction to Stata for Health Researchers** (5th ed., 2020)
*Svend Juul and Morten Frydenberg*

Clear, example-driven introduction to Stata's workflow and syntax. Excellent for absolute beginners, with careful attention to data management, graphics, and reproducible research practices.

**Maximum Likelihood Estimation with Stata** (5th ed., 2018)
*William Gould, Jeffrey Pitblado, and Brian Poi*

Essential for researchers who need to write custom estimators. Comprehensive coverage of Stata's `ml` command, the `mlexp` interface, and Mata implementation. Includes worked examples for logit, probit, survival models, and complex likelihood functions.

## Specialized Topics

### Causal Inference and Program Evaluation

**Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion** (2009)
*Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke*

The essential guide to credible causal inference in applied research. Focused on design-based identification strategies including IV, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences. Written with clarity and practical wisdom from two Nobel laureates.

**Counterfactuals and Causal Inference: Methods and Principles for Social Research** (2nd ed., 2015)
*Stephen L. Morgan and Christopher Winship*

Comprehensive treatment of the potential outcomes framework, combining formal theory with applied examples. Excellent for understanding matching, weighting, instrumental variables, and sensitivity analysis from a counterfactual perspective.

**Causal Inference for Statistics, Social, and Biomedical Sciences: An Introduction** (2015)
*Guido W. Imbens and Donald B. Rubin*

Authoritative presentation of the Rubin Causal Model and modern approaches to causal inference. Rigorous yet accessible, with careful attention to assumptions, identification, and estimation. Essential reading for anyone conducting program evaluations or treatment effect studies.

**Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference** (2nd ed., 2009)
*Judea Pearl*

Groundbreaking introduction to causal inference using directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Provides a formal framework for thinking about confounding, mediation, and selection bias. Fundamental for understanding structural approaches to causality.

### Panel Data and Longitudinal Methods

**Econometric Analysis of Panel Data** (6th ed., 2021)
*Badi H. Baltagi*

The leading textbook for graduate courses in panel data econometrics. Comprehensive coverage of fixed effects, random effects, dynamic models, limited dependent variables, spatial panels, and nonstationary panels. Clear exposition with extensive examples.

**Analysis of Panel Data** (3rd ed., 2014)
*Cheng Hsiao*

Rigorous treatment of panel data methods with strong emphasis on economic theory and identification. Particularly valuable for understanding dynamic panel models, sample selection, and measurement error in longitudinal contexts.

### Survival Analysis and Event History

**An Introduction to Survival Analysis Using Stata** (3rd ed., 2016)
*Mario Cleves, William Gould, and Yulia Marchenko*

The essential Stata guide for time-to-event analysis. Covers the Kaplan-Meier estimator, Cox proportional hazards, parametric models, competing risks, and frailty models. Clear examples with detailed Stata code.

**Event History Modeling: A Guide for Social Scientists** (2004)
*Janet M. Box-Steffensmeier and Bradford S. Jones*

Accessible introduction to event history analysis for social science applications. Covers discrete-time, continuous-time, and competing-risks models with practical guidance on model selection and interpretation.

**Applied Longitudinal Data Analysis: Modeling Change and Event Occurrence** (2003)
*Judith D. Singer and John B. Willett*

Outstanding treatment of growth curve modeling and hazard analysis. Exceptional pedagogical approach with real datasets and intuitive explanations. Particularly valuable for understanding time-varying covariates and model building strategies.

### Time Series Econometrics

**Time Series Analysis** (1994)
*James D. Hamilton*

The classic graduate-level reference for time series econometrics. Comprehensive coverage of ARMA/ARIMA models, vector autoregressions (VAR), cointegration, and state-space methods. Rigorous and encyclopedic.

**Applied Econometric Time Series** (4th ed., 2015)
*Walter Enders*

Practical, accessible treatment of modern time series methods. Excellent for understanding unit roots, cointegration, VAR models, and ARCH/GARCH volatility models. Strong emphasis on applied examples and Stata/R implementation.

### Spatial Econometrics

**Introduction to Spatial Econometrics** (2009)
*James P. LeSage and R. Kelley Pace*

The definitive introduction to spatial econometric methods. Covers spatial lag, spatial error, and spatial Durbin models with clear explanations of spatial weights matrices and interpretation of effects. Includes MATLAB code and practical applications.

**Spatial Econometrics: Methods and Models** (1988)
*Luc Anselin*

The foundational text that established spatial econometrics as a field. Still essential for understanding maximum likelihood estimation of spatial models and formal testing procedures.

### Bayesian Econometrics

**Bayesian Econometrics** (2007)
*Gary Koop, Dale J. Poirier, and Justin L. Tobias*

Accessible introduction to Bayesian methods for econometricians. Covers prior specification, MCMC algorithms, model comparison, and applications to linear regression, panel data, and limited dependent variables.

**Bayesian Econometric Methods** (2nd ed., 2019)
*Joshua Chan, Gary Koop, Dale J. Poirier, and Justin L. Tobias*

Comprehensive graduate-level treatment with extensive Matlab code. Covers modern computational methods and applications to macroeconometrics, time series, and microeconometrics.

## How to Use This Library

### For Beginners
Start with Wooldridge's *Introductory Econometrics* for foundations, complemented by Kennedy's *Guide to Econometrics* for intuition. Use Cameron and Trivedi Volume I for Stata implementation.

### For Graduate Students
Combine Wooldridge's *Cross Section and Panel Data* or Greene's *Econometric Analysis* with Cameron and Trivedi's two-volume set. Add specialized texts (causal inference, panel data, time series) based on your research needs.

### For Applied Researchers
Use this library as a reference: consult journal articles for method-specific guidance, verify implementation with Cameron and Trivedi, and check theoretical foundations in Greene or Wooldridge as needed.

### For Instructors
The textbooks are organized by pedagogical level (introductory -> advanced) and topic. Journal articles provide real-world examples of method application and common pitfalls to avoid.
